# Variants considered

Nine one-line candidates, all 1–200 characters, judged against the
published brief in order: on-subject, warmth, surprise, one concrete
detail, fresh wording.

## Winner

**I — pigeons (131 chars, picked)**

> The old man who always shouts at pigeons in the park pressed a warm bun into my hand this morning and said, 'eat, you're too thin.'

Why it wins: warmth is real (a stranger's maternal kindness, not a
reported sentiment); surprise is the strongest turn of the nine (the
man known for shouting at pigeons does something tender, and the
kindness arrives as a scolding); concreteness is triple (pigeons in the
park, a warm bun, the quoted words); wording is its own rhythm, no
greeting-card phrasing. The humour lands the smile the bounty asks for.

## Shortlist

- **A — warung (137 chars, runner-up).** "At 6 a.m. the warung lady slipped an extra krupuk into my bag and whispered, 'for the road, dik' — I walked home taller than the sunrise." Warmest of the nine and very concrete, but "taller than the sunrise" is slightly odd and "krupuk / dik" may read as opaque to the judge.
- **G — grumpiest man (134 chars).** "The grumpiest man on my street, who has never once said hello, held the lift door for me this morning and said, 'after you, sunshine.'" Strong surprise, but "after you, sunshine" leans sitcom-like; less warm than the winner.
- **B — cat (144 chars).** "My neighbour's cat, who has ignored me for two years, headbutted my ankle this morning and purred like a rusty motorbike starting on a cold day." Best surprise after the winner, but cat affection carries less human warmth than a stranger's kindness.
- **D — umbrella (123 chars).** "A stranger at the traffic light handed me his umbrella in the rain, then sprinted off laughing, already soaked to the skin." Punchy and surprising, but the warmth is thinner and there is only one concrete detail.
- **H — bread (128 chars).** "My 4 a.m. alarm went off and I groaned — then saw my neighbour had left warm bread at my door with a note: 'for the early bird.'" Balanced, but the turn is gentler than the winner's and the note is a familiar device.
- **C — bus (134 chars).** "The bus driver waited while an old man counted his coins with shaking hands, then waved the fare away and said, 'today's on the road.'" Warm, but the closing line is close to greeting-card phrasing.
- **E — vendor (142 chars).** "The street vendor remembered my order before I spoke, then threw in an extra fritter and a grin that cost him nothing and bought my whole day." Warm and concrete, but the surprise is small.
- **F — recipe (123 chars).** "I found my grandmother's recipe card tucked inside a cookbook today, and her handwriting still smelled faintly of cinnamon." Warm and concrete, but bittersweet rather than smile-first, and the surprise is low.
